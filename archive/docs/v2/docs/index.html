The Story & Philosophy

Why pure JSON transformation requires separating the Food from the Recipe.

1. The Mental Model: The Food and the Recipe

When working with enterprise systems like Tally or SAP, data arrives in rigid, verbose structures. Transforming this data into clean models often degenerates into brittle JavaScript loops and mutable scripts.

json-transformer solves this with a clear mental model:

  • structure.json is the Food: The raw, untouched client data. It represents the source of truth from an external system.
  • data.json is the Recipe: A clean, declarative JSON structure defining your transformation instructions.
  • json-transformer is the Kitchen: It takes the food, reads the recipe, and produces the transformed dish without ever modifying the original ingredients.

2. Why Two Separate Files? The Immutability Principle

A natural question arises: "Why maintain two separate files? Why not embed transformation directives directly inside the source JSON?"

Embedding directives inside source data is a dangerous anti-pattern for three critical reasons:

  1. Client Data is Sacrosanct: You do not own the source data. Mutating incoming client payloads pollutes shared references, invalidates caches, and creates subtle side-effects across your application.
  2. Preventing Infinite Loops: If the traversal engine were to mutate or enrich the source object while recursively navigating it, it would create self-referencing branches and trigger infinite loops.
  3. Different Lifecycles: Client data changes on every HTTP request or database query. The transformation recipe changes only when your business contract evolves. Decoupling them keeps your architecture deterministic and pure.

3. The Recipe is Knowledge, Not Duplication

Developers sometimes ask: "Why does the recipe mirror the hierarchy of the source JSON? Isn't that duplicate effort?"

No. The recipe is not duplication—it is proof of domain knowledge.

You cannot reliably reshape complex enterprise data (like Tally batches or ledgers) without first understanding its hierarchy. When an engineer constructs the recipe matching the path down to the target field, they are formally codifying:

  • Structural Proof: "I understand that STOCKITEM contains BATCHALLOCATIONS.LIST, which contains GODOWNNAME."
  • Intentionality: Only paths that are explicitly acknowledged and intended for transformation are altered. Everything else is safely left untouched.
  • Protection from Sloppy Hacks: It prevents developers from taking lazy shortcuts or applying unvalidated transformations. The recipe is a disciplined, typed blueprint of intent.

4. The Architecture at a Glance

The transformation engine operates as a pure function:

import transform from "@keshavsoft/json-transformer";

const result = transform(source, recipe);

It takes two inputs, applies declarative directives recursively, and returns a new transformed JSON structure with zero side effects.
